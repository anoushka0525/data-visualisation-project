THE BIG PICTURE

More than gadgets. Everyday infrastructure.

Household appliances have reshaped everyday routines, changing how people store food, wash clothes and manage household activities. Yet access to these appliances differs across countries and categories. Using International Energy Agency household appliance data, this website investigates how adoption and ownership patterns have evolved. A second dataset covering appliance operating costs introduces another perspective: the electricity-related expenses associated with using household appliances.

THE TAKEAWAY

The modern home is changing.
Ownership is only part of the story.

Appliance ownership data allows us to investigate adoption trends, international differences and changes in installed appliance stocks. Operating-cost data adds a complementary perspective by examining how electricity expenses differ between appliance categories and models. Together, these perspectives highlight the importance of considering both appliance access and energy-related costs. However, the two datasets describe different populations and measures. They cannot establish direct causal relationships between national ownership patterns and individual appliance running costs.

03 · Methodology and limitations

IEA observations may differ by country, category and year. Operating costs depend on electricity tariffs, usage assumptions and model efficiency. Missing values are not treated as zero. Comparisons between the two datasets are descriptive, not causal.
