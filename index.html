From refrigerators to dishwashers, the appliances inside our homes tell a story about convenience, access, and consumption. Explore how ownership patterns evolve—and what data can (and cannot) tell us about consumer choice.

THE BIG PICTURE

More than gadgets. Everyday infrastructure.

Household appliances affect how we store food, clean clothes and organise daily life. Adoption is not uniform: appliance type, country, and year all matter. This visual story first explores ownership with IEA data, before turning to product listings and consumer ratings when a separate product dataset is added.

Diffusion measures average appliances per dwelling. A value of 1.0 means one appliance per dwelling on average—not necessarily that every household owns one.

03 ANOTHER LENS

Ownership is more than
a single statistic.

Penetration reports the share of households owning an appliance. It is conceptually different from diffusion and cannot be directly combined on one numeric scale.

PERCENTAGE · HEATMAP

Where are appliances widely owned?

For economies with penetration records, colour represents the latest reported household ownership percentage.

Countries and years are restricted by data availability. White / blank cells indicate missing observations, not zero ownership.

THE TAKEAWAY

Appliance adoption is global.
Consumer choice is personal.

The IEA data lets us compare long-run household appliance uptake, but ownership metrics cannot establish purchasing motivations, product quality or brand preference. Combining adoption data with properly sourced product listings lets us explore both perspectives without confusing them.

Methodology

Stocks are expressed in millions of units; diffusion is average units per dwelling; penetration is a proportion. Values are not interpolated, and cross-country latest-year comparisons may mix years. Retail product rating data does not measure sales volume.
