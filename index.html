THE BIG PICTURE

More than gadgets. Everyday infrastructure.

Household appliances have transformed the way people store food, wash clothes and manage everyday activities. However, appliance adoption differs significantly across countries and appliance categories. Using data from the International Energy Agency, this visualisation explores how household appliance ownership has developed over time, how adoption varies across countries, and how appliance stocks relate to household diffusion.

03 HOUSEHOLD OWNERSHIP

Ownership is more than
a single statistic.

Appliance penetration measures the proportion of households that own a particular appliance. Unlike diffusion, penetration describes household ownership rather than the average number of units per dwelling.

THE TAKEAWAY

Appliance adoption is global.
But ownership patterns differ.

The International Energy Agency's household appliance data provides insights into changes in appliance diffusion, ownership penetration, and installed appliance stocks across countries. Together, these visualisations allow us to investigate trends over time, compare international patterns, and explore how appliance adoption differs by category. However, ownership and stock measures alone cannot explain purchasing motivations, customer satisfaction, brand preferences or product quality.

Sources and limitations

01 · International Energy Agency

International Energy Agency, Household Appliances Database, June 2026 pilot edition. The dataset contains appliance ownership and stock observations across countries over the period 2000–2024.

IEA Dataset ↗

02 · Methodology

Three measures are used throughout the visualisation: Diffusion represents the average number of appliances per dwelling. Penetration represents the proportion of households owning an appliance. Stocks represent the installed number of appliances in millions of units.

03 · Data Limitations

Data availability varies by country, appliance category and reporting year. The latest available observations are not necessarily from the same year. Appliance ownership and stocks do not directly measure sales, customer ratings, prices or purchasing motivations.
