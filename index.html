THE BIG PICTURE

More than gadgets. Everyday infrastructure.

Household appliances have changed how people store food, wash clothes and manage everyday household activities. However, the availability and ownership of these appliances differ significantly across the world. Using data from the International Energy Agency, this visualisation examines appliance adoption over time and compares ownership patterns across countries. A second dataset covering appliance running costs provides another perspective on the electricity-related expenses associated with household convenience.

01 APPLIANCE ADOPTION

How appliances become
part of everyday life

Appliance diffusion measures the average number of appliances per dwelling. A diffusion value of 1.0 represents one appliance per dwelling on average, rather than ownership by every household.

TIME SERIES · MULTI-LINE

How has appliance diffusion changed over time?

Explore how different appliance categories have become more widespread over time. Select a country and hover over the lines to investigate changes in diffusion.

Key insight — Australia's changing homes: Between 2000 and 2023, television diffusion increased from 3.89 to 5.85 units per dwelling, while PC and ICT diffusion nearly doubled, from 1.41 to 2.79. Dishwasher diffusion also increased from 0.32 to 0.73 units per dwelling. These changes suggest that some household technologies became substantially more common, while others experienced slower growth.
Source: IEA Household Appliances Database. Australia, 2000–2023. Select Australia in the country dropdown to view these figures.
COMPOSITION · STACKED AREA

How has installed appliance stock changed?

Explore how the total number of appliances in use has changed. Each coloured area represents a different appliance category.

Key insight — More appliances in Australian homes: Between 2000 and 2023, Australia's installed television stock increased from approximately 28.32 million to 60.99 million units. Over the same period, dishwasher stock increased from 2.35 million to 7.60 million units, more than tripling. These trends show that the total number of appliances in use has grown substantially, even when changes in average appliances per household are more modest.
Source: IEA Household Appliances Database. Australia, 2000–2023. Select Australia in the country dropdown to view these trends.
02 GLOBAL APPLIANCE PATTERNS

One world.
Different levels of access.

Household appliance ownership varies significantly across countries and regions. Geographic visualisations help reveal where appliances are widely adopted and where the total number of installed appliances is greatest.

GEOGRAPHIC · CHOROPLETH MAP

Where are clothes washers more common?

Countries are shaded according to the average number of clothes washers per dwelling. Darker blue indicates higher diffusion, helping reveal differences in appliance adoption across the world.

Key insight — Not all households have the same level of appliance access: In 2023, Greece recorded approximately 1.22 clothes washers per dwelling, compared with 1.03 in Australia and 0.71 in Brazil. This geographical variation highlights differences in appliance adoption between countries.
Source: IEA Household Appliances Database. Country comparisons use the latest available observations from 2015 onwards. Grey countries represent unavailable or unmatched data, not zero ownership. Reporting years may differ.
GEOGRAPHIC · PROPORTIONAL SYMBOL MAP

Where are the most clothes washers installed?

This map compares the estimated number of clothes washers in use across countries. Larger circles indicate greater installed appliance stocks. Unlike diffusion, total stocks are strongly influenced by the size of a country's population and number of households.

Key insight — Population size influences appliance stocks: China recorded approximately 560.16 million installed clothes washers in 2024, compared with 109.21 million in the United States. The substantial difference demonstrates why total appliance stock should not be confused with household adoption. Countries with larger numbers of households may have significantly more appliances in use, even when their diffusion rates are not the highest.
Source: IEA Household Appliances Database. Latest available observations from 2015 onwards. Reporting years differ, and circle positions are approximate.
03 HOUSEHOLD OWNERSHIP

Ownership is more than
a single statistic.

Appliance penetration measures the proportion of households that own a particular appliance. Unlike diffusion, which measures the average number of units per dwelling, penetration describes how widespread ownership is.

GEOGRAPHIC · SPIKE MAP

Where are refrigerators owned by more households?

This geographic spike map illustrates refrigerator ownership across countries. Taller orange spikes represent higher percentages of households owning refrigerators. Hover over a spike to inspect the recorded ownership percentage.

Key insight — Refrigerator ownership varies across countries: Refrigerator penetration shows the percentage of households that own a refrigerator. Higher penetration indicates more widespread ownership, regardless of the total number of households in a country. The geographic spikes highlight differences in refrigerator ownership across countries, providing a clearer picture of household access than total appliance stock alone.
Source: IEA Household Appliances Database. Latest available observations from 2015 onwards. Reporting years may differ, and spike heights are schematic rather than geographic distances.
04 UNDERSTANDING DIFFERENCES

How different are
our appliance habits?

Beyond examining appliance ownership individually, we can investigate changes over time, differences between countries and relationships between appliance stock and diffusion.

CHANGE · DUMBBELL CHART

How much has appliance diffusion changed?

Compare the earliest and latest recorded diffusion values across appliance categories in Australia. Each connecting line illustrates the overall change between the two observations.

Key insight — Appliance adoption changes at different rates: Australia's appliance diffusion patterns show that different household technologies have experienced varying degrees of change over time. Categories with longer connecting lines indicate larger differences between their earliest and latest recorded values, while shorter lines suggest relatively stable diffusion. These comparisons highlight how some appliances have become more common in Australian homes, while others have experienced smaller changes.
Source: IEA Household Appliances Database. Australia, earliest versus latest available observations. Reporting periods may differ between appliance categories.
DISTRIBUTION · BOX PLOT

How much does appliance diffusion vary across countries?

Box plots compare the spread of appliance diffusion values across countries. Each box shows the middle half of observations, while the central line represents the median. Points beyond the whiskers represent potential outliers.

Key insight — Appliance diffusion varies across countries: The distribution of appliance diffusion reveals differences in how widely household technologies are adopted across countries. Categories with wider boxes show greater variation among the middle 50% of countries, while narrower boxes indicate more consistent diffusion levels. Outliers highlight countries with unusually high or low appliance diffusion compared with others.
Source: IEA Household Appliances Database. Latest available observations from 2015 onwards. Reporting years may differ between countries.
DISTRIBUTION · SANKEY DIAGRAM

How are installed appliance stocks distributed across countries?

This Sankey-style diagram compares installed appliance stocks across appliance categories and the six countries with the largest reported stock totals in 2022. The width of each connecting band represents the number of appliance units in use, measured in millions. Hover over the bands to explore the reported quantities.

Key insight — Appliance stocks are unevenly distributed: The Sankey diagram reveals how installed appliance stocks are distributed across appliance categories and the six countries with the largest reported stock totals in 2022. Wider connecting bands represent larger numbers of appliances in use, highlighting differences in the composition of appliance stocks between countries. These differences may reflect household population size, appliance adoption patterns and the availability of reported data.
Source: IEA Household Appliances Database, 2022. Values are measured in millions of units. The bands represent a breakdown of installed stocks, not physical movement of appliances between countries.
05 THE COST OF CONVENIENCE

Appliances make life easier.
But what do they cost to run?

Ownership reveals how widely appliances are adopted. Operating-cost data provides another perspective by showing the estimated annual electricity expenses of different appliance categories. The following charts use a separate dataset of certified appliance models.

COMPARISON · BAR CHART

Which appliance categories have the most model records?

Compare the number of appliance model records in the operating-cost dataset. This reveals the distribution of records across categories, rather than product popularity or market sales.

Key insight — Appliance categories have different dataset coverage: Refrigerators account for the largest number of records in the WattWise dataset, with approximately 1,000 entries, followed by dishwashers with 709 records. Televisions have the fewest records among the eight categories, with 172 entries. This uneven distribution highlights differences in dataset coverage, which should be considered when comparing operating costs across categories.
Source: WattWise Labs Appliance Running Costs dataset. Values represent dataset records, not product sales, market share or the number of appliances owned by households.
RANKING · LOLLIPOP CHART

Which appliances have higher estimated annual running costs?

Clothes dryers have the highest median estimated annual electricity cost in the dataset, at approximately US$113. Washing machines have the lowest, at approximately US$20. This difference highlights how electricity costs vary considerably across appliance categories.

Values are in US dollars and reflect the source's operating-cost assumptions. They should not be interpreted as Malaysian household electricity bills.
VARIATION · RANGE CHART

How much can operating costs vary within appliance categories?

Each horizontal line connects the minimum and maximum estimated annual operating costs within an appliance category. The orange dot represents the median annual cost. This makes it easier to identify categories with unusually wide cost ranges.

Minimum-to-maximum ranges may be influenced by extreme values. These are dataset-level estimates, not distributions of household bills.
THE TAKEAWAY

The modern home is changing.
Ownership is only part of the story.

Household appliance data reveals variation in the adoption and use of everyday technologies. Geographic comparisons highlight differences in appliance diffusion, household penetration and the total number of installed appliances. Operating-cost data adds another dimension by showing how electricity expenses vary across appliance categories and models. Together, these visualisations demonstrate why both accessibility and operating costs are important when examining modern household appliances. The datasets describe different populations and measures, so the comparisons should be interpreted descriptively rather than causally.
